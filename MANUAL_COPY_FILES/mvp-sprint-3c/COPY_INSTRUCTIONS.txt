THE BUG YOU FOUND
─────────────────
  A tenant onboarded for lifts AND HVAC got a first contract containing every
  block of both — roughly 166 rows, ~140 of them spare parts including ₹35k
  rescue devices. That was this screen pre-ticking everything shortlist()
  returned. My defaulting decision, and wrong: it holds for one equipment's
  services and collapses at two equipments plus a parts catalog.


1. ONE EQUIPMENT AT A TIME
──────────────────────────
  VaniCandidate already carries `equipment`, so the screen groups by it and
  asks which one this contract is for. The picker only appears when there is
  more than one — a single-equipment tenant sees no extra control.

  Switching equipment re-defaults the ticks to that equipment's services.


2. SERVICES TICKED, PARTS NOT
─────────────────────────────
  Within an equipment, services and spare parts are now separate sections.
  Services are ticked by default; parts are listed dashed and unticked, with
  the note "charged when used, so normally left out of an AMC".

  A service is identified by carrying a cadence (cycle_days > 0). For an
  Elevator / Lift the split is about 12 services to 70 parts, which is exactly
  why parts must not be ticked by default.

  Parts are capped at 12 with a line saying how many more are in the catalog —
  a first contract does not need a 70-row parts list, and silently truncating
  without saying so would be worse.


3. THE VARIANT IS NAMED — BUT NOT PRICED
────────────────────────────────────────
  "Your catalog covers 8 types of Elevator / Lift. This quick contract uses
   Traction Passenger Elevator (Geared) — the full wizard lets you pick any of
   them per contract."

  Read from the blocks' variant_pricing, because the composer's candidate
  payload drops variants entirely (vaniComposerService never mentions the
  word). Purely additive: if that query fails the line is omitted.

  ONLY THE NAME IS USED, NEVER THE VARIANT PRICE — and this is worth your
  attention independently of onboarding:

    block                                base_price   1st variant price
    Electrical & Controls Maintenance      ₹1,000            19
    Hoistway & Pit Servicing               ₹1,200           960
    Ride Quality & Mechanical Inspection   ₹1,500             1

  Those read like multipliers or bad seed data rather than rupees (there is an
  m_kt_variant_price_multipliers table, which would explain it). Quoting them
  could produce a ₹20 annual AMC, so the contract stays on the block's own
  price. ALSO NOTE: t_contract_blocks has NO variant column — a contract never
  records which variant it is for. The variant is a catalog-authoring concept,
  so naming it is the honest limit of what this screen can claim.

  OPEN QUESTION FOR YOU: if a tenant picks a variant in the full wizard today,
  is it charging ₹1 for a mechanical inspection? That is a live-catalog
  question, not an onboarding one, and it is not touched here.


4. EVENT SCHEDULE AND CONTRACT DOCUMENT — BY REUSING VaNiReviewFinalize
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  I said I would read it before building a parallel one. I did, and it does
  the entire second half already:

    - renders ReviewSendStep      the contract document view
    - renders EventsPreviewStep   the service + billing timeline
    - performs the submit itself
    - shows the success state with the CNAK and a copy button
    - takes exactly one input: a VaniComposeResult

  A VaniComposeResult is precisely what assemble() returns. So this screen now
  collects the answers, calls assemble(), and hands the result over.

  Consequences:

    * The duplicate submit() this file used to carry is DELETED. There is now
      one submission path for the express flow, the wizard and the composer.
    * The express flow shows the same document, schedule and CNAK treatment as
      the composer, because it IS the same component.
    * A bug is fixed on the way: submit() returns global_access_id and the
      previous version discarded it — the express flow minted a CNAK and never
      showed it to anyone.
    * NOTHING IS WRITTEN until the tenant has seen the document and the
      schedule. Previously the contract was created and then they moved on.

  The button changes from "Create in test mode" to "Review the contract →",
  because that is now what it does.

  The framing you asked for sits on the form, before the button:
    "Next you'll see the contract itself and its full schedule — every service
     visit and every invoice, dated. That is the point of doing this once: your
     delivery calendar and your receivables are planned from the day the
     contract starts."
