1. THE VENDOR QUOTE PAGE   /quote/:cnak/:secret
───────────────────────────────────────────────
  Public, no auth, outside the app shell. The vendor is a CONTACT, not a
  tenant — there is no account to log into, now or later.

  Built dependency-light on purpose: no ThemeContext, no admin components,
  bare axios with no auth interceptors. That is the same call SessionCheckinPage
  made for logged-out members on a phone, and this is the second surface of
  exactly that kind. Same palette, same shell proportions.

  The vendor sees: what is being asked for, the line items, the term, and the
  nomenclature (AMC / CMC / …).
  The vendor does NOT see: the buyer's own prices, or the other vendors.
  Both are withheld SERVER-side in rfq_resolve_for_vendor — the page could not
  leak them even if it wanted to.

  They answer one of three ways:
    * one total figure
    * a price per line (total computed live, and it is what gets sent)
    * "I'm not quoting on this", with an optional reason

  Revising is a first-class action, not an edge case. A vendor who already
  quoted lands on the page with their figure, notes, validity date and any
  per-line prices pre-filled, and the button reads "Update my quote". Making
  them retype a quote they already gave is the fastest way to lose one.


2. THE BUYER'S SIDE — RfqQuotesPanel
────────────────────────────────────
  The read side already half-existed: VendorsCard rendered name, status and
  quoted_amount. What was missing was ordering, the breakdown, and the act of
  choosing. This is a separate component so VendorsCard is untouched for
  ordinary contracts, which still just list vendors.

    * cheapest quote first, then unanswered, then declines; an awarded vendor
      always leads regardless of price
    * a "lowest" tag when more than one vendor has quoted
    * "opened the link" on a vendor who has viewed but not answered — that is
      the viewed_at column batch 1 added, and it is the difference between
      "they are ignoring me" and "it never reached them"
    * per-line breakdowns expand inline
    * decline reasons shown, because "outside our area" is worth knowing

  THE CONFIRM STEP SAYS WHAT AWARDING ACTUALLY DOES:

    "This does not create a contract. The vendor raises the contract from
     their side, and it will carry the figure you just agreed."

  A buyer clicking a button labelled "Award" will otherwise assume a contract
  now exists. It does not, and that is deliberate — your model is that the
  vendor initiates. This is the one place a user could form a wrong belief
  about it, so the sentence is on the confirm step rather than in a doc.
