  You were right that it is a "no page found", not an HTTP error.

  src/pages/onboarding/steps/LovSetupStep.tsx exists — 440 lines, with a
  default export — but App.tsx NEVER IMPORTED IT and never declared a route
  for it. The /onboarding parent lists 24 children; lov-setup is not among
  them.

  Five places navigate there anyway:

    TermsConditionsStep.tsx:147,158   persona !== 'both' -> lov-setup
    Screen8BEquipmentStep.tsx:154,161 continue and skip
    ResourcePickStep.tsx:252          the Skip branch
    pages/onboarding/index.tsx:57     the resume map

  No child route matches, so React Router falls through to
  App.tsx:910 — <Route path="*" element={<NotFoundPage />} />.

  This explains every symptom exactly:

      creation by the `tenants` edge function, not by this screen

  /onboarding parent, which already carries ProtectedRoute requireTenant and
  OnboardingLayout. No guard changes.

3. THE NUMBERING — THREE MODELS, ALL DISAGREEING
────────────────────────────────────────────────
  You asked why the numbering is inconsistent. There were three separate
  step models running at once:

  (a) OnboardingLayout's header dots + counter, driven by
      OnboardingUtils.getAllSteps() — the LEGACY 11-step list:
        welcome, storage-setup, user-profile, theme-selection,
        business-basic, business-branding, served-industries,
        business-preferences, sequence-numbers, master-data, complete
      Almost no VaNi route is in it, so findIndex returned -1 and the header
      counter rendered "0 / 11" with NO dot marked current. The dots were
      also clickable and navigated into the legacy flow.

  (b) Hardcoded "Step N of 9" strings inside six VaNi components:
        userProfileStep:239        Step 2 of 9
        BusinessDetailsStep:139    Step 3 of 9
        PersonaSelectionStep:304   Step 4 of 9
        ThemeSelectionStep:295     Step 5 of 9
        IndustrySelectionStep:358  Step 6 of 9
        VaniConsentStep:240        Step 7 of 9 · VaNi Consent — 6A
      A different total, on a different scale, from the header above it.

  (c) The express rail — its own three-step model.

  So a screen could truthfully claim to be step 0 of 11 and step 7 of 9 at
  the same time. That is what you were looking at.

  ONE MODEL NOW: src/components/onboarding/journey.ts, rendered by ONE
  component, src/components/onboarding/JourneyRail.tsx, used by BOTH the
  express screens (via ExpressShell) and the OnboardingLayout header.

  The journey branches exactly as the code does — read off the components,
  not assumed:

    VaniWorkingStep:390     buyer -> equipment-confirm, else -> pricing-review
    Screen8APricingStep     -> terms-conditions
    TermsConditionsStep:147 both  -> equipment-confirm, else -> lov-setup
    Screen8BEquipmentStep   -> lov-setup
    LovSetupStep            -> done

  giving:

    seller  Your business · What you service · Building · Your prices ·
            Your terms · Your lists · Done                        (7 steps)
    buyer   Your business · What you service · Building ·
            Your assets · Your lists · Done                       (6 steps)
    both    Your business · What you service · Building · Your prices ·
            Your terms · Your assets · Your lists · Done          (8 steps)

  THE LONG FORM IS UNTOUCHED. resolveJourney() returns null for any route
  outside the express journey, and OnboardingLayout then renders exactly its
  original markup. The six hardcoded "Step N of 9" strings are all on
  long-path-only screens now, so they were left alone — see OPEN ITEMS.

  journey.ts and JourneyRail.tsx live in components/onboarding/, NOT in
  src/lite, because src/lite may import outward but nothing outside it may
  import in (the eslint no-restricted-imports rule from Sprint 1). A shared
  model has to sit outside for OnboardingLayout to use it.
